PROJECT 03 / ANTIBODIES

Antibody Design

Interface-guided VHH design for OX40.

This project explores the redesign and computational assessment of an INBRX-106-related VHH domain. Short peptides are screened against an OX40 competitive interface, incorporated into VHH constructs and reviewed across human and cynomolgus receptor models.

The aim is to prioritize candidates using converging evidence from sequence integrity, interface geometry, model consistency and development risks. The supplied report compares CAND-0136 and VHH-150 as candidates for experimental follow-up.

Scope: computational candidate prioritization for a VHH domain. Binding, competition and function remain to be established experimentally.

CAND-0136

First experimental priority in the report, based on broader CDR2 contact coverage and more passing models across both species. Competition evidence remains limited.

Human models passing: 3/5 · Cyno models passing: 2/5. Reported multi-model ipTM means: 0.416 ± 0.206 / 0.457 ± 0.150 (human / cyno).

VHH-150

Retained as an alternative with a more hydrophobic and aromatic sequence profile. Its higher best cyno score is not representative of the full model ensemble.

Human models passing: 1/5 · Cyno models passing: 1/5. Reported multi-model ipTM means: 0.238 ± 0.175 / 0.299 ± 0.204 (human / cyno).

How the work progressesPeptide library → competitive interface → VHH construct → cross-species review

From short-peptide screening to VHH candidates

The workflow below follows the supplied OX40 report. L3 is sequence-level construction; L4 is complex-structure review.

  1. 01

    Parent-domain definition

    Start from the report’s 1D10v6 parent VHH domain (115 aa). Record framework boundaries, CDRs and terminal sequence before design.

  2. 02

    Short-peptide pool

    Generate or collect 7–15 aa peptides. Preserve batch identity and sequence provenance rather than treating repeated records as unique molecules.

  3. 03

    Competitive-interface screen

    Apply the report’s L2 criteria: hotspot ≥3, core ≥1, interface residues ≥2, BSA ≥200 Å² and no severe clashes.

  4. 04

    VHH sequence construction

    Replace the complete CDR2 segment and audit sequence junctions, framework retention and terminal KP. Verify all CDR changes against the parent.

  5. 05

    Human / cyno refolding

    Review multiple complex models for human and cynomolgus OX40 separately. The candidate summaries show five models per species.

  6. 06

    Interface and sequence-risk review

    Evaluate ipTM, buried area, hotspot/core contacts, CDR2 coverage and clashes alongside charge, hydrophobicity and sequence liabilities.

  7. 07

    Evidence-based prioritization

    Use tier, passing-model counts, interface coverage and sequence risks together. The report prioritizes CAND-0136 before VHH-150.

  8. 08

    Experimental follow-up

    Confirm binding, competition and function experimentally. Evaluate stability and aggregation risks; single-model confidence is insufficient.

Evaluation MetricsInterfaces, model ensembles and sequence-level risks
Interface confidenceipTM · Mean ± SD · Best model+

Keep best-model and multi-model summaries separate. ipTM is interface confidence, not Kd, binding strength or agonist activity.

Interface geometryBSA / Å² · Hotspot/core · Severe clashes+

The L2 report specifies BSA ≥200 Å², hotspot ≥3, core ≥1, interface residues ≥2 and severe clash count = 0. These are project-stage criteria, not universal antibody thresholds; do not assume they describe all L4 rules.

Cross-species consistencyHuman / cyno pass counts+

Report each species and its denominator. A pass in one sampled model does not demonstrate robust binding across the full ensemble or across species.

CDR2 contributionContact coverage · Residue contact pairs+

The comparison reports 7/12 CDR2 coverage and 45 residue contact pairs for CAND-0136, versus 5/12 and 37 for VHH-150. These are model-derived interface observations.

Molecular propertiesLength / aa · MW / kDa · pI · Charge at pH 7.4+

Preserve molecular units, pH and sequence definition. CAND-0136 is reported as more acidic; VHH-150 is reported as more basic. Charge differences do not establish potency.

Sequence and development riskGRAVY · Aromaticity · Instability index · Cys · Glycosylation motifs+

Use these as sequence-level screening descriptors. Review hydrophobic patches and flagged DG/deamidation liabilities experimentally rather than treating a motif flag as measured degradation.

Competition and functionModel gate versus experimental blocking+

The report’s competition evidence is limited. Prioritized candidates require binding, competition and functional assays; the reviewed isolated VHH domain does not by itself establish the activity of the full INBRX-106 format.

Source reconciliationConstruction rules and candidate annotations+

The workflow states CDR1/CDR3 retention, but candidate tables display CDR1/CDR3 sequences different from the parent. The CAND-0136 slide also contains a conclusion paragraph referring to VHH-150. The page follows the tabulated comparison and flags these discrepancies for reconciliation before construct finalization.

Validation priorities

The report recommends testing CAND-0136 first and retaining VHH-150 as an alternative. Follow-up should establish binding, competitive effects and function, together with sequence-specific stability and aggregation behavior. Candidate construction records and the source discrepancies should be reconciled before finalizing experimental constructs.
