RESEARCH / MEDICINE

Medical research.

Immunology. Metabolism. Aging. Virtual biology.

Four connected directions for understanding biological systems and developing testable research ideas. Our approach brings together genomic evidence, molecular design and computational models, with experimental validation guiding interpretation.

Immune recognition

Research themes

  • Cyclic peptide and antibody design

03 / AGING

Biological change
across time.

Explore age-associated changes in cellular states and molecular networks, with an emphasis on separating descriptive associations from causal mechanisms.

Research themes

  • Age-associated gene expression and cellular-state variation
  • Relationships among metabolism, immune regulation and aging
  • Data-driven prioritization of mechanisms for follow-up
  • Study designs that account for tissue, cohort and technical effects

Methods and resources

AI in Genomics — evidence integration and hypothesis generation →Education — research practice and study resources →

Aging is presented as a research direction. No rejuvenation effect or validated intervention is claimed here.

04 / VIRTUAL BIOLOGY

Models that connect
data and experiments.

Develop computational representations of biological systems to explore cell states, organize evidence and generate predictions that can be tested in the laboratory.

Research themes

  • Virtual-cell data resources and state representations
  • Genomic and transcriptomic modeling
  • Perturbation-oriented questions and evaluation design
  • Feedback between computational hypotheses and experiments

Related projects and resources

AI in Genomics — data-to-hypothesis workflows →Virtual Cell Library — browse cells and expression ↗Virtual Screening Library — sequence and evidence search ↗

The published libraries currently provide data browsing and candidate preparation. They do not execute cell simulation, molecular docking or experimental validation.

A shared research approach.

  1. Define a biological question.
  2. Identify suitable data and evidence.
  3. Build or apply a fit-for-purpose model.
  4. Review uncertainty and alternative explanations.
  5. Test the prediction and refine the question.
