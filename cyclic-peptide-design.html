PROJECT 02 / CYCLIC PEPTIDES

Cyclic Peptide Design

Connecting molecular topology, target recognition and developability.

Our cyclic peptide project combines reference-led chemical redesign with de novo backbone and sequence design. Two complementary case studies address IL-23R and the IL-17 family.

The workflow brings together full chemical definitions, complex prediction, epitope analysis, property assessment and iterative candidate review. Monocyclic designs and a PEG-linked two-ring scheme are evaluated with their distinct structural assumptions kept explicit.

These are computational research workflows from the supplied project reports. Predicted confidence, permeability and stability are not experimental efficacy or pharmacokinetic measurements.

How the work progressesIL-23R reference-led redesign · IL-17 de novo design

Route A / IL-23R: scaffold modification and reassessment

A reference-led route starting from icotrokinra and extending two selected candidates at six positions.

  1. 01

    Reference & candidate generation

    Start from the reference scaffold and retain the original generation and selection records. Historical tools, weights and cutoffs still require verification.

  2. 02

    Sequence-risk mapping

    Use PeptideCutter on natural-residue proxy sequences to identify protease-recognition hotspots. Proxy counts are not actual cleavage predictions for modified cyclic peptides.

  3. 03

    Complete chemical definition

    Use pyPept and RDKit to encode modified residues, stereochemistry, N-methylation and head-to-tail closure. Validate valence and connectivity.

  4. 04

    Complex prediction

    Use Boltz-2 with three samples per molecule in the reported reassessment. Apply geometry checks before selecting representative models.

  5. 05

    Interface assessment

    Compare ipTM, target-epitope coverage, contact precision and buried SASA against the reference using the same calculation settings.

  6. 06

    Property assessment

    Calculate MW, heavy atoms and TPSA with RDKit. Assess predicted serum stability and Caco-2 permeability using the stated PeptiVerse endpoints.

  7. 07

    Joint candidate review

    Review 2D chemistry and 3D poses together. Balance binding-site recognition, polarity and stability without inventing universal hard cutoffs.

  8. 08

    Next validation

    Prioritize revisions and experimental follow-up. The supplied comparison does not establish an overall improvement over icotrokinra.

Route B / IL-17: de novo design and three-form review

An eight-stage route covering monocyclic candidates and a PEG-linked two-ring design.

  1. 01

    Target & epitope definition

    Use PDB 9SGH, 8RUU and 5N92 to define the design region and shared epitope. Retain the mapped residue set as the contact reference.

  2. 02

    Backbone generation

    Generate head-to-tail cyclic peptide backbones with RFdiffusion and RFpeptides, recording the intended ring length.

  3. 03

    Geometry filtering

    Apply project-specific checks for ring closure, backbone geometry and receptor-surface compatibility before sequence design.

  4. 04

    Sequence design

    Use ProteinMPNN with cyclic positional encoding. Record residue identity and any proposed noncanonical modifications.

  5. 05

    Complex prediction

    Predict peptide–target complexes with Boltz-2 and aggregate ipTM over the defined samples or clusters.

  6. 06

    Interface review

    Analyze epitope contacts and per-side buried SASA. Reject or repair geometrically problematic poses before interpreting their interfaces.

  7. 07

    Patent review

    Search sequences and claims as a supporting review step. Keep structure and source records; a database search does not establish freedom to operate.

  8. 08

    AA / AF / FF transfer

    Transfer and predict candidates for IL-17AA, IL-17AF and IL-17FF separately. Report form-specific confidence and interface measures.

Two-ring chemistry requires a complete linker definition

The BC-7-PEG20 scheme joins two head-to-tail cyclized 22-residue peptide arms using PEG20 diacid. Its ends form amide bonds with the side-chain ε-amines at P-Lys2 and Q-Lys9. Full-molecule descriptors must include that linker.

The supplied complex predictions contain the receptor and two peptide chains without explicit PEG atoms. Their geometry cannot demonstrate preservation of the pose after full-linker modeling or establish bivalent affinity.

ToolsMethods documented in the supplied project reports
Design & sequence generationRFdiffusion · RFpeptides · ProteinMPNN+

Backbone generation, cyclic design and sequence proposals in the IL-17 workflow. These methods do not replace explicit chemical validation of modified residues.

Chemical structure definitionpyPept · RDKit+

Encode complete connectivity, closure and stereochemistry. Compute full-molecule descriptors; include the PEG linker for the two-ring molecule.

Complex modelingBoltz-2+

Predict target–peptide poses. Keep sampling, geometry checks, selection criteria and aggregation rules explicit.

Interface analysisProject contact scripts · Biopython ShrakeRupley+

Compute epitope coverage and precision from defined residue sets and contact cutoffs, and calculate SASA with a consistent convention.

Property predictionPeptiVerse · Project HGB model+

The IL-23R report uses PeptiVerse serum-stability and Caco-2 endpoints. The IL-17 report uses an internal HGB permeability regressor trained on CycPeptMPDB; its PAMPA endpoint differs and the candidate estimates are out of domain.

Protease risk mappingExPASy PeptideCutter · Project sequence rules+

IL-23R uses natural-residue proxies with cross-enzyme deduplication. IL-17 uses heuristic trypsin, chymotrypsin and elastase rules. These outputs are not direct degradation half-lives.

Structure visualizationUCSF ChimeraX+

Inspect and render predicted complexes; distinguish reference structures, modeled poses and 2D chemical definitions.

Evaluation MetricsConfidence, interfaces, chemistry, stability and permeability
Interface confidenceBoltz ipTM · Sampling and geometry+

Report sampling and summary statistics. ipTM measures model interface confidence, not measured affinity, potency or target inhibition.

Epitope contactsCoverage · Precision · Distance cutoff+

For IL-17, the report defines coverage as hit E1 residues / 21 on the main binding chain, and precision as E1 hits / contacted receptor residues. Contacts use a heavy-atom distance ≤4.5 Å. These are project-defined calculations.

Buried surface areaSASA / Å² · Total versus per-side+

The IL-17 report uses per-side burial = (SASA receptor + SASA peptide − SASA complex) / 2. IL-23R tables report total buried SASA. Do not compare these numbers without reconciling the convention.

Molecular size and polarityMW / Da · Heavy atoms · TPSA / Å²+

Calculate from complete chemistry. Lower TPSA or larger buried area alone does not establish improved permeability or binding.

Ring topology and residue compositionRing length / aa · Noncanonical residues · Linker+

Record each ring separately for two-ring designs, as well as closure type, modifications and linker chemistry. Sequence alone is insufficient to specify all chemical features.

Permeabilitylg Papp · Assay-specific endpoint+

Record units (Papp in cm/s), assay endpoint and model domain. The reports use different Caco-2 and PAMPA prediction routes; IL-17 values are out-of-domain extrapolations and should not be used for confident ranking.

StabilityPredicted t½ / h · Serum versus plasma+

Keep serum stability, plasma stability and human elimination half-life separate. The IL-17 report lists recent stability models as alternatives, not the confirmed provenance of all existing values; retain that uncertainty.

Protease susceptibilityProxy or heuristic cleavage-site counts+

Count only under stated rules and topology conventions. Terminal markers and omitted closure bonds must be recorded. Noncanonical residues, N-methylation and cyclization limit sequence-proxy interpretation.

Cross-form and experimental reviewIL-17AA / AF / FF · Follow-up assays+

Evaluate each target form independently. The report flags an atom-overlap problem in a No42 FF representative model. Geometry must be resolved before biological interpretation; binding and function require experimental testing.

Evidence and next steps

Candidate review should integrate chemical integrity, plausible target geometry and comparable endpoint definitions. The supplied reports identify trade-offs and unresolved checks; they do not support a general claim of improved affinity, oral exposure or long-acting behavior.
